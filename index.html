HOW TO PLAY

Follow what the record proves.

Each person is connected to one item of evidence. Use the clues currently on the docket to establish whether an item was admitted or excluded.

  1. Find a ruling that follows with certainty from the visible clues.
  2. Choose Admitted or Excluded on that person's card.
  3. A correct deduction reveals a new clue and opens the next part of the case.
No guessing required. If the record cannot support your choice yet, it stays unmarked. Contradictions are explained without a penalty.

Two kinds of reasoning

The logic clues tell you what the fictional judge ruled. After each deduction, the Civil Litigation Explanation shows the simplified legal reason for that ruling. Rows, columns, neighbors, and board position are puzzle mechanics. They are not legal grounds for admitting or excluding evidence.

Discovery is broader than trial evidence. An item may be discoverable even if it is not ultimately admitted at trial. The parties may collect and review evidence during discovery that the judge later excludes from the trial record. This game focuses on trial admissibility.

In Evidence uses simplified fictional facts and rules for classroom learning. Real evidentiary decisions depend on the jurisdiction, purpose for which evidence is offered, objections, foundation, and particular facts of the case.
